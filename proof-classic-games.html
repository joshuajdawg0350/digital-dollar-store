Proof, not promises

How the Classic Games Pack was tested.

Our rule: if it's for sale, it has to work — and there has to be proof it works. So before this pack went on the shelf, every game was played, end to end, with real taps — a test driver tapped the screen the way your finger would, in a real browser engine. The games' insides were only ever read to check the score, never used to move a piece. Below is the actual record from November 17–18, 2026: what was played, what passed, what broke and got fixed — and at the bottom, the honest list of what has not been checked yet.

PROOF▾

♞ Chess

10 of 10 tests passed.

  • Played to the end, three ways: Fool's Mate tapped out move by move and the game declared mate; a random-move human against the Medium computer ended in checkmate in 58 halfmoves; Hard vs Medium computer game ended in a draw in 84 halfmoves.
  • Full rules, proven by taps: castling (including refusing to castle through check while queenside castling worked in the same position), en passant (the passed pawn was really removed), promotion with a chooser — including underpromoting to a knight — plus stalemate, insufficient material, and threefold / 50-move draws.
  • The computer actually plays: on Medium it took a free queen when one was offered, and saved its own queen by winning a rook. Its longest think on Hard measured 1,983 ms (capped under 2 seconds).
  • Broke and fixed during testing: the computer once searched the live board and leaked its own moves (now it searches a copy); a bad piece-value table once blinded it to a free queen; thinking once overran its clock. All three were found by the tests, fixed, and re-tested.

⛂ Checkers

35 checks passed, 0 failed.

  • Played to the end: computer vs computer (Medium vs Medium) ran to a draw by the 80-halfmove rule at 157 halfmoves; a human side against Easy ended Black wins at 41 halfmoves, 8 pieces to 0.
  • American rules, proven by taps: a forced jump could not be refused (a quiet move was rejected); a multi-jump kept the turn until the chain finished and took both pieces; a man jumping into the king row crowned and ended the turn; a blocked player with no moves lost, as the rules say; the draw fired at exactly 80 halfmoves.
  • Speed: the Hard computer's opening move computed in 137 ms.
  • Broke and fixed during testing: the computer once moved behind the title screen before the game started (a title gate was added); a race in the computer-vs-computer driver was fixed.

♠ Solitaire (Klondike)

19 of 19 checks passed — every move by real taps.

  • The win path is proven twice: a nearly-won layout was finished with 12 manual foundation taps (13/13/13/13, win banner, and the win counter went up and stayed up after reload) — and a second game was won with the AUTO-FINISH button.
  • A full real game, reported honestly: on a fresh shuffled deal (seed 7, Draw 1), an autoplayer made 126 moves over 600 taps with zero crashes and a consistent game state throughout. It did not win, and it was not stuck — so no win is claimed for that deal. That's the standard here: we report the game we actually got.
  • Rules, proven by taps: same-color placement rejected, alternating colors accepted, only a King may fill an empty column, wrong foundation cards rejected, Ace to foundation by double-tap accepted. Undo five times restored the exact earlier game state (verified by state fingerprint); the deck held exactly 52 unique cards after three consecutive fresh deals.
  • Layout: no overflow at phone sizes 390×844 and 360×740.
  • Broke and fixed during testing: the AUTO-FINISH button once rendered at zero size so the first test tap hit nothing; a double-tap branch was unreachable; card art overlapped the corner numbers. All fixed and re-tested.

▦ Crosswords

All 6 puzzles verified, then solved through the real screen — 1,084 letters typed.

  • Content checked by an independent verifier (word list of 9,858 common English words): every entry is a real word, every crossing agrees, every letter cell is crossed both ways, exactly one clue per entry, no one-letter entries, nothing shorter than 3 letters. 156 everyday words, zero crossword slang; all 262 clues were written by hand to match each word. The six puzzles: Morning and At the Beach (11×11, 44 entries each), Around the House and Animals (10×11, 37 each), Road Trip (12×11, 51), In the Kitchen (12×11, 49).
  • Every puzzle was then completed through the actual game screen — cell taps and on-screen keyboard taps only: 1,084 letters typed, the solved state fired on all six, and every solve was unassisted (assisted = 0). Progress persisted: after a reload the picker showed SOLVED ✓ 100%.
  • Negative tests: a wrong letter gets flagged by Check Letter; Check Puzzle with errors does not declare a solve (92 cells flagged in the test); fixing the letters clears the flags. Using Reveal Letter marks the solve as assisted — and the win screen says so, honestly.
  • Broke and fixed during testing: the header overflowed at phone width and clipped the mute button; on the tallest puzzles the keyboard's bottom row was cut off at 360×740. Both fixed and re-checked clean.

What has NOT been checked yet — the honest list

  • Not yet played on a real phone. All of the above was played in a desktop browser engine at phone sizes. The games have not yet been played on an actual Galaxy A26 (or any physical phone) — that check is still owed, and if your phone shows us something the tests missed, the Fix-It Guarantee below is how we make it right.
  • Sound has never been heard. The games' beeps and clicks are generated in code and the mute buttons were exercised in testing, but the test setup has no ears — audibility is unverified.
  • Chess and Checkers were not screenshot-checked at the smallest phone size (360×740). Solitaire and Crosswords were, and both needed — and got — fixes there. Chess and Checkers use fluid layouts and were checked at 390×844; the 360×740 screenshot check for those two is still owed.
  • Solitaire has no dragging. Tap-to-move only, by design (see the record above). And one honest gap inside the testing itself: a naturally stuck full-length Solitaire deal was never observed during the runs — the stuck-detection and its message were proven separately, but seed 7's game ended at the tap cap, still moving.
  • Landscape / rotation was not part of the pass for Checkers and Crosswords; the games are built and checked as portrait phone games.

Every game also switches skins mid-game without disturbing the game in progress (asserted in testing), remembers your pick between visits, and makes zero network requests — the whole pack works with the internet off. When any item on the not-checked list gets checked, this page gets updated with what we found — including if it's bad news.

Update — November 18, 2026: graphics polish, same games

The pack got a standards-and-graphics pass: colors unified to the house chrome/silver + electric blue (#0096FF), chrome button treatment, clearer focus outlines, and bigger tap targets. Only the paint changed. We verified that every game's program code is byte-for-byte identical to the build tested above — no rules, moves, or puzzle code were touched — and re-ran a live browser check of the polished pack: all four games and the pack menu load clean, respond to taps, and show zero errors. The test record above therefore stands for this build.
