Proof, not promises

How Hustle City Solitaire was tested.

Our rule: if it's for sale, it has to work — and there has to be proof it works. This is the real test record for the exact build going on the shelf (BIG v2, November 21, 2026): a complete game played to a win with real taps, the card and number sizes measured on screen, and the owner's own hands on his own phone. Hits and misses both — including the one phone where this build first rendered wrong, the bug we found behind it, and how that story ended. It's all below, in plain words.

A full game, played to a win — real taps, no shortcuts

✓ PASS — complete win at 390×844, 52 of 52 cards home

  • The whole game, start to finish: a natural deal (seed 4) played end-to-end at 390×844 with 546 real taps — 254 moves and 38 stock draws — ending on the game's own win screen: "YOU WIN — Solved in 292 moves," with all 52 of 52 foundation cards home. The win screen was captured from the finished game, not mocked up.
  • Second phone size: at 360×740 the same build ran 9 moves and 5 stock draws clean.
  • Zero page errors across every test run at both sizes.
  • Before the tap replay, the winning line was found with an exact replica of the game's own dealing and rules, then verified inside the real game — so the win you see is the real game being won, not a rigged demo.

The big cards — measured, not promised

✓ PASS — numbers and letters up 75% in the artwork; cards at 95–97% of what a phone screen physically allows

This build exists for one reason: a real player told us the earlier cards were too small to read. So the faces were redrawn at 840×1260 with plain white faces and a thin border only, and the corner numbers and suits were enlarged by a measured 75%:

  • Why the cards themselves didn't grow 75%: solitaire lays seven columns side by side. On a 360 px-wide phone screen, seven cards with literally zero gap between them max out at 51.4 px per card — a 75% wider card does not fit on any phone without sideways scrolling, and we won't sell a card game you have to scroll sideways. This build sits at 95–97% of that physical ceiling: full-width board, gaps cut to 2 px, taller cards. The numbers and letters — the part you actually read — got the full 75% and then some on screen.
  • The card backs are the original Hustle City backs, unchanged byte for byte. The rules and gameplay are unchanged — only the faces, sizes, and layout moved.

The honest limits — read these before you buy

  • The one phone that rendered it small and blurry — RESOLVED, and she passed it. The player this big-card build was made for (Cheryl) first reported that on her phone the new build still looked too small and kind of blurry, even though the same build rendered correctly on the owner's phone and laptop. Her verdict on the earlier build was a plain fail — "That is worse. I cant read them" (November 21, 2026) — and that failed check is exactly why BIG v2 was built. We dug in and found the cause on our side: the game was holding onto an old saved copy on phones that had opened it before, so the bigger cards may never have reached her — she was judging the previous build. That is fixed (the game now checks for the newest version on every visit instead of serving the saved copy first; live-verified November 21, 2026). She re-checked it after the fix and passed it the same day — her verdict, relayed by the owner: "Cheryl's game is good." The fail stays on this page because it happened; the fix and her pass are on it too.
  • Card width is capped by the screen, as measured above. If your phone is narrower than 360 px, cards will be proportionally smaller; the corner numbers scale with them.
  • Not yet checked: a purchase by a real buyer. We never fake a sale, so the first genuine checkout is the end-to-end proof of the payment step itself. Everything up to that step — this page, the game, the price — is published here, and the Fix-It Guarantee below covers the rest.

Verdict: TESTED AND MEASURED — PASS, November 21, 2026, on build BIG v2 — the same build you get when you buy. If a future check finds something these tests missed, this page gets updated with what we found — including if it's bad news.
