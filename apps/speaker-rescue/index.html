Set the phone up first

  • Speaker facing down, so water can fall out of the grille.
  • Grille clear — nothing pressed against it, no finger over it.
  • Take off any case that covers the grille. A covered grille traps the water in.
  • Turn the media volume up before you start — the tones work at the volume you set.

What this can and can't do — plain words

Sound waves can help move surface water and dust out of a speaker grille. That is all this does.

It cannot repair water damage inside the phone, and it cannot fix a torn or blown speaker.

No result is guaranteed. If the speaker still sounds muffled after two full cycles, the problem is hardware, not water in the grille — see When this can't help.

How it works

  1. Tap Record “before.” The app plays a 3-second test sweep (250–1,200 Hz) and records it with the mic.
  2. Run a Water Eject or Dust cycle.
  3. Come back and tap Record “after” — same spot, same volume, same quiet room.

Read this before you trust the numbers

These readings are indicative, not a lab measurement. They are computed from the actual recording your mic made — Level is how loud the sweep came back, Clarity is where the sound's energy centered (a muffled, water-blocked speaker usually reads lower on both). Room noise, mic position, and distance all change the numbers, so compare two readings taken the same way — never treat one reading as a verdict.

The mic is used only when you tap Record. Recordings are analyzed right here on this phone and never leave it.

When this can't help

Tones move air. They cannot repair parts. If any of these sound like your phone, stop running cycles and save your money for the fix that actually works.

It's hardware if…

  • The speaker crackles or distorts at every volume — that is usually a torn or blown speaker driver.
  • The phone went fully under water, or water got inside past the grille — internal water damage is not something sound can push out.
  • It's still muffled after two full Water Eject cycles. Two honest tries is the test. More cycles won't change a hardware problem.
  • There's no sound from the speaker at all — a dead driver or a loose connection, not a blocked grille.
  • Charging, buttons, or the screen misbehave after the phone got wet — water is inside the phone. Power it off and get it looked at.

The honest summary

Sound waves can help eject surface water and dust from a grille. They cannot repair internal water damage or a blown driver, and no result is guaranteed — anyone who promises otherwise is selling you something.

If the speaker is torn or blown, a repair shop can replace it. Knowing when to stop is worth more than another ten cycles.

Before the first run — these tones are loud

  • The eject tones play at the volume you set, and low tones at full volume are loud.
  • Hold the phone away from your ears while a cycle runs — and away from pets' ears too.
  • Never run a cycle with earbuds in or headphones on.
